# Project 2 Design Summary

The routing table is a statically allocated array indexed by the 8-bit destination ID (256 slots). Each entry stores validity, selected next hop, hop count, age since its last selected-next-hop update, and an unreachable withdrawal timer. The node's own route is always valid at cost 0. Neighbor discovery keeps at most 32 entries and confirms a bidirectional link with a beacon, acknowledgment, and confirmation. Every confirmed neighbor has a direct route of cost 1. Excess neighbors are logged and ignored.

Distance-vector control packets use AM type 7 and are sent directly to one neighbor, independently of the data route. Each packet reuses the fixed 28-byte `pack` format. Its payload contains a count and up to nine (destination, cost) byte pairs. A staged sender emits one page every 250 ms and retries a page if the SimpleSend queue is full. It sends all known finite and withdrawal entries to each neighbor; omission in one page does not change a route. Recipient-specific poison reverse advertises 16 for destinations whose selected next hop is that recipient, while the self-route is always 0.

The hop metric is bounded by `INFINITY = 16`; addition is checked before incrementing. Equal-cost advertisements keep the current valid next hop. A selected next hop can raise or withdraw its route; worse candidates from other neighbors are ignored. Each learned route ages independently and expires after 600 seconds without a refresh. Neighbor loss invalidates every route using that next hop immediately after the neighbor table expires (5 missed 1-second beacons). Newly unreachable entries remain advertised as 16 for 1200 seconds, then may be reclaimed; a newly learned finite route cancels withdrawal.

A 1-second age timer starts the next staged round when neighbors exist and the previous round is complete. This avoids an all-at-once send burst and continues periodic refreshes. The tradeoff is that a large route table or many neighbors lengthens a full round. There are 256 route slots, 32 neighbor slots, 9 entries per DV packet, and a 20-message send queue/pool per SimpleSend component.

Ping and ping reply share the existing network header and use AM type 6 for unicast next-hop forwarding. End-to-end source, destination, protocol, and sequence remain unchanged. The source starts TTL at 15; a relay drops TTL <= 1 and otherwise decrements once. Destination delivery requires positive TTL. Flooding remains in its own service and is selected only for radio broadcasts; routed unicast packets never consult the flood duplicate cache and never fall back to flooding.
